## Your First Hour: A Practical Beginner Checklist

The single biggest mistake new players make in small indie adventures is rushing. These games are usually built around exploration and puzzle-solving, and the content is dense relative to its size. Spend your first hour establishing good habits instead of chasing progress.

Here's a sequence that works well for a first session:

| Step | What to do | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Adjust settings before you start playing | Small indie titles often ship with default sensitivity, volume, or resolution settings that don't suit everyone |
| 2 | Explore the opening area fully before advancing | Early areas usually teach mechanics you'll need later |
| 3 | Read every note, sign, and dialogue line | Narrative clues often double as puzzle hints |
| 4 | Check your journal, inventory, or quest log regularly | Objectives can update silently after events |
| 5 | Save often if manual saving is available | It's the cheapest insurance against a lost hour |
| 6 | Keep a written note of anything that looks important | Codes, symbols, and locked doors often come back later |
| 7 | Avoid walkthroughs until you're genuinely stuck | Puzzle satisfaction is the main value of the genre |

### Habits That Pay Off in Story-Driven Indie Games

- **Backtrack deliberately.** When you gain a new ability or item, revisit earlier areas — this is a near-universal design pattern in exploration games.
- **Talk to every character twice.** Dialogue often changes after story beats, and second conversations carry new information.
- **Look up and down.** Environmental details above and below eye level are where hidden paths usually live.
- **Don't hoard consumables.** Save them for genuine difficulty spikes, but don't finish the game with a full inventory either.
