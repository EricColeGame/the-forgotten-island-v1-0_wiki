## How to Play: Controls, Vitality, and Stamina

The control scheme is intentionally minimal. Almost everything on the island is handled with two keys, which makes the learning curve close to zero. The trick is understanding that vitality and stamina are two separate resources, and they recover in completely different places.

| Action | Input | What It Does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recharge vitality | Hold "E" beside food or a bed | Restores your health pool |
| Recharge stamina | Hold "E" beside a well | Restores the resource combat drains |
| Fight an NPC | Hold "F" beside an NPC | Starts a battle with that character |
| Improve your build | Level up and buy stronger equipment | Raises your combat ceiling |

A few practical habits make the early game much smoother:

- **Top off vitality before you explore.** Food and beds are your health stations, so treat every meal as a checkpoint.
- **Never start a fight on an empty stamina bar.** Attacking costs stamina, and a tired character is a dead character.
- **Use the well regularly.** Stamina refills are free and fast, and there is no reason to walk into a brawl at half capacity.
- **Bank your talent points with a plan.** Randomly spreading points across three stats produces a character who is mediocre at everything.

Community reports suggest that new players who ignore the well for their first hour end up losing fights they should have won. It sounds obvious once you have played for a while, but the game never spells it out for you.

## Combat Rules and the Talent System Explained

Combat in The Forgotten Island v1.0 runs on a small set of rules, and every one of them is a trade-off. There is no free action. Attacking costs stamina, defending costs nothing but deals no damage, and relaxing restores stamina while leaving you completely open.

| Combat Action | Effect | Stamina Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light attack | Reduces enemy vitality | Yes |
| Heavy attack | Reduces enemy vitality (stronger) | Yes |
| Defend | Blocks an incoming attack | None |
| Relax | Restores stamina, but you cannot defend | None |

There is one more rule that catches people out: if you are attacked by an enemy, your recovery stops working. In practice, that means you cannot simply out-heal a fight. You have to read the enemy's rhythm, defend when they swing, and relax only when you have a safe window.

### Strength, Agility, or Intelligence?

Every talent point you earn converts into a fixed set of stat bonuses. There is no randomness here, so you can plan a build in advance:

| Talent Point | Bonuses Gained |
| --- | --- |
| 1 Strength | +120 Vitality, +50 Stamina |
| 1 Agility | +5 Attack, +2 Armor |
| 1 Intelligence | +20 Crit, +20 Avoid, +40 Lucky |

Reading the table, three archetypes emerge naturally:

- **Strength builds** are the safest choice for new players. The vitality and stamina bonuses directly offset the cost of attacking, letting you fight longer before you need a well.
- **Agility builds** trade durability for raw damage and a little armor. They suit players who defend well and want fights to end quickly.
- **Intelligence builds** are the gamble. Crit, avoid, and lucky stats scale in unpredictable ways, but a high-intelligence character can dodge and crit their way through encounters that would flatten a strength build.

If you are unsure, put your first several points into Strength. The extra stamina alone pays for itself in every single fight.

## Farming, Fishing, and Exploration Tips

The combat system gets the most attention, but the island itself is the real selling point. The store description frames the game around a simple question — can you find a way back to the real world? — and the answer involves talking to everyone and poking at everything.

| Activity | Why It Matters | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Farming | Steady source of food | Supports vitality recharging |
| Fishing | Additional food supply | Pairs well with farming |
| Eating and drinking | Restores vitality | Core survival loop |
| Talking to merchants | Equipment and supplies | Upgrading gear raises your combat ceiling |
| Talking to villagers | World context and tasks | Helps you complete each level |
| Exploring buildings | Progress toward going home | The main narrative thread |

A few exploration habits that player experience consistently endorses:

1. **Talk to every NPC you pass.** Merchants and villagers are the two character types on the island, and both of them have something useful to say or sell.
2. **Do the assigned tasks before you wander.** Each level is completed by finishing the tasks you are given, so clearing those first keeps your progression moving.
3. **Fish and farm in the same session.** Both feed the eating and drinking loop, which is what keeps your vitality topped up between fights.
4. **Buy equipment as soon as you can afford it.** Stronger gear is the most reliable power spike in the game, and it costs nothing but time.

The pacing is gentle by design. The store page notes that players can engage in farming, fishing, eating, and drinking — and that it is entirely up to them to discover their own way of enjoying the game. Treat the island as a place to hang out rather than a challenge to beat, and it holds up much better.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is The Forgotten Island v1.0 really free to play?**
Yes. The base game is free to download and play on Steam. The only paid content is the Premium pack, which adds more characters for $1.99.

**What are the controls in The Forgotten Island v1.0?**
Hold "E" beside food or a bed to recharge vitality, hold "E" beside a well to recharge stamina, and hold "F" beside an NPC to start a fight. Everything else is leveling, shopping, and exploring.

**Which talent should I level first?**
Strength is the safest starting choice because it grants 120 vitality and 50 stamina per point. Agility suits aggressive damage builds, while Intelligence is best for players chasing crit, avoid, and lucky stats.
